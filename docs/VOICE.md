# VOICE.md — how Repeater speaks

This is the wording constitution for every public page. The structure may be
rebuilt; this voice may not be diluted. When in doubt between two sentences,
the plainer, shorter, more factual one wins.

## The position

Repeater is the established operator. The reader is a business owner who is
considering working with us — the site is written from our side of the desk,
calm, and certain, the way a successful firm writes. We never rub anything in,
never brag, never compete for the reader's sympathy. We state what we sell,
what it costs, and what it does; the reader decides whether they qualify to
apply. Confidence here is *understatement with numbers*, not adjectives.

Concretely:

- **Prices on the page.** Every hard figure is printed. Nothing is "contact
  us for pricing" except where the price genuinely depends on volume (AI
  answering is quoted per business — say that plainly and give the setup-call
  path, don't dance around it).
- **Terms are fixed.** One phone plan per client, 24-month term, minimum two
  devices, caps and fees printed in a table. Fixed terms read as strength;
  flabby "we'll work something out" reads as weakness.
- **The client applies to us.** CTAs: "Book the setup call", "Apply for a
  trade account", "Start the free trial". Not "Sign up now", never urgency
  devices ("limited spots", "today only").
- **We say no plainly.** A solo tradie with one handset is steered to a
  walk-in repair — that honesty is a flex; keep it.

## Hard bans (a line containing these fails review)

- Hype and AI-slop adjectives: *revolutionary, game-changer, cutting-edge,
  supercharge, unlock, unleash, elevate, seamless, effortless, empower,
  delight, supercharge, world-class, best-in-class, next-level*.
- **No rhyming, no swinging rhythm, no clever wordplay, no alliteration
  trios.** No "Screens. Smashed? Sorted." — that is exactly the register we
  are leaving behind.
- No rhetorical questions outside the FAQ blocks.
- No exclamation marks. Anywhere.
- No "we're passionate about…", "founded on the belief that…", "in today's
  fast-paced world", "look no further".
- No em-dash confetti: a sentence earns its punctuation; most should be short
  sentences separated by full stops.
- No emoji, no decorative symbols, no "✨".

## Rhythm and shape

- Short declarative sentences. Then a longer one when the detail genuinely
  needs room. Vary length by meaning, not by ornament.
- Numbers carry the persuasion. "$622 all-in per device", "2 service events
  per quarter", "$60 flat past the cap" — print them, don't adjectivise them.
- Second person for the reader's business ("your crew", "your phones"), third
  person for us ("Repeater supplies…", "the AI answers…"). We are spoken
  ABOUT more than we speak ABOUT ourselves.
- Australian English (organise, colour, metre). en-AU locale everywhere.
- Honest hedges only where truth demands them: "usually same day", "quoted
  against your call volume". Never hedge what is fixed; never fix what is
  hedged.

## The three-line self-description (the spine of every page)

1. Phones for crews, with the repairs handled.
2. An AI that answers the calls nobody can.
3. The operating system that runs the shop behind both.

Any page's first screenful should be traceable to these three lines. Trade
supply of parts is depth behind the products — one line, never the headline.

## Jarvis's spoken register (tour calls and the phone persona)

Same rules aloud, minus typography: short, flat, unhurried; numbers spoken
cleanly ("six twenty-two, all in, per device"); never sells past the question;
offers the role-play once, plainly, and takes no for an answer. He never says
"awesome", never laughs, never does enthusiasm. The one warmth allowed: a
clean goodbye that thanks them for the time.
